    A finished example

    The sample guide is a small finished guide with a module, a lesson and blocks of the kinds you will use most. It is added to the guide you have open.

    Keyboard shortcuts

    The main ones. Help > Keyboard keys (Ctrl+/) lists them all.

    • Ctrl+K Search for a command
    • Ctrl+/ The list of all the keys
    • F10 Go to the menus, and back to where you were
    • Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y Undo and redo (Edit menu)
    • Ctrl+Alt+=, Ctrl+Alt+- and Ctrl+Alt+0 Zoom the page in, out and back to its actual size

    Your own cues

    Name up to three cues of your own, such as Debrief. They are kept in this document and appear in the cue list under "For you". Each has a picture of its own.

    Activity library

    Choose an activity and it goes into your guide after the block your cursor is in, written as labelled parts: objective, materials, preparation, instructions and, if it uses one, a case study. Change the words afterward, in Word or with Edit.

      Your activities

      Put your cursor in an Activity you wrote, then choose Save. It is kept on this computer and is here in every guide you open.

        Slides from a presentation

        Put a presentation's slides into your lesson, each with its number, title, animation clicks, speaker notes and, if you give them, its picture. Choose the presentation; for the pictures, choose a PDF of it (PowerPoint: File, Save a Copy, PDF) or the slides saved as pictures.

        Slides to insert

        The slides go after the block your cursor is in. Hidden slides are not chosen unless you choose them. A slide with no matching picture gets its title, clicks and notes only.

        Update from slides

        You changed the presentation. Choose it again; for the pictures, also choose a new PDF of it (PowerPoint: File, Save a Copy, PDF). Only what differs from the guide is listed, and anything unchanged is left alone. A slide that moved or was renamed in PowerPoint is still found.

        Changes to bring in

        Speaker notes you rewrote in Word are kept unless you tick them. A slide that moved is renumbered where it is; it is not moved in the guide. New slides go in after the slide before them. Nothing is changed until you choose Update the guide, and Ctrl+Z in your document takes it back.

        E-Learning screens

        For designing E-Learning screen by screen. Add screen puts in the usual blocks (visual direction, on-screen text, narration) with a sample sentence under each; type over the sentences. The narration is counted and timed at 150 words a minute.

        Makes a zip with a project for OpenStage Author: a slide for each screen, with its heading, on-screen text and narration, and a notes file with everything else the storyboard says. Unzip it, then open the folder that ends in .osproj in OpenStage Author.

                      The correct number above is for the key. Give the number alone, like 42 or 3.5. If an answer within 0.5 counts, type 0.5 here. The units, like kg, are told to the learner on the line they write the answer on.

                      Separate the item from its match with | or a tab, so a line pasted from a spreadsheet works. The choices are listed in alphabetical order, so the order of the items does not give the answers away.

                      Rating scale

                      3 to 8 items. In the document they are listed mixed up, with letters, and the key gives the right order.

                      Add points after a | if you want them, like: Listens without interrupting | 2. The checklist goes in the key, for the facilitator. The learner gets the task and lines to write on.

                      Add points after a | on a level line if you want them, like: Meets | 2. Each criterion line has the criterion and then one description for each level, in the same order, separated by |, like: Listens | Never interrupts | Interrupts once or twice | Often interrupts.

                      Like: Boring | Interesting. Separate the two words with | or a tab.

                      Paste many questions

                      Paste questions in the plain-text layout test banks use. They go in the section your cursor is in, after the item your cursor is in, and are numbered to follow the questions already there. Nothing is added unless every question reads; the ones that do not are named.

                      What the layout looks like

                      Each question starts with its number. Options are lettered, with a * before the correct one. Put a Type: line before a question that is not multiple choice: MA (more than one correct), TF (true or false), E (essay), S (short answer), F (fill in the blank, the options are the accepted answers) or MT (matching, each option written like a. Term = Meaning). An Answer: line after the options, or an Answers: list at the end, can give the key instead of the *. A Points: line before a question gives its points. Titles, feedback lines and publisher notes are skipped.

                      Type: MA
1) Which of these are noble gases?
*a. Neon
*b. Argon
c. Oxygen

2) Sound travels faster than light.
a. True
*b. False

                                                              KEEP A RECOVERABLE MASTER
                                                              Backups and restore
                                                              Keep the full Word master, including formatting, manual additions, images, course records, and teaching assets. Backups contain facilitator instructions and answers.
                                                              Save a checkpoint
                                                              Finish the current operation first. A backup captures the checked Word master; it does not replace saving your working document.
                                                              Verify and restore
                                                              Choose a saved OpenStage backup. After verification, download its preserved master as a newly named Word copy. Open that copy in Word and save it separately. Supporting assets remain in the backup package.
                                                              Verification checks package integrity; it does not certify who created a file. This prototype supports masters up to 64 MB and backup files up to 80 MB. Restore prepares a separate copy and leaves the current guide in place.
                                                              

                                                              Support and recovery
                                                              Keep a copy of your guide's file before you try something new. The OpenStage Guides folder also keeps the last three dated copies of each guide.
                                                              Build and support information
                                                              Check loaded build at the top of the pane to compare observed code identities with the local server snapshot. Unknown, stale or mixed code does not qualify a current build for acceptance. The download includes build hashes and capability flags; it excludes document text and paths, absolute filesystem paths, settings, private notes and user identity. It performs no repair or configuration changes. Keep your work before any later reload. Native Word and manifest checks remain separate.
                                                              After an uncertain save or interrupted operation, course changes and previews pause. Preserve a copy first. Recovery checks every managed field and updates course records only when the insertion is complete. It never inserts the content again or deletes incomplete work. Missing, duplicated, or unexpected fields require preserving the file and returning to a previously saved copy. You can restore a previously verified backup from Backups. Save the Word document after successful recovery.
