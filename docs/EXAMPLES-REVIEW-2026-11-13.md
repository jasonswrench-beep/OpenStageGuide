# What a range of real training documents shows we may have missed

2026-11-13. Draft for the author's review. Nothing here is built.

## What was looked at

The author supplied 16 facilitator guides, trainer manuals and workbooks (11 PDFs and 5 older Word files, 39 MB, 11 to 301 pages): a one-hour micro-training guide, a five-day workshop guide, a human-centered-design workshop guide, a self-study slide deck, a flight-simulator instructor manual, a standards-based professional-learning guide, a South African unit-standard guide, a team-model workshop excerpt, a coaching-style theory excerpt, a workshop guidebook made in a cloud word processor, a guide about how to use training modules, and five classic "program" files (overview, objectives, outline table, directions, reproducible forms A to J).

Method: four reviewers each read a few documents against a list of what the Guide can express today (61 cues, activity parts, 15 assessment item types, the workbook, timing, delivery versions, slide import, e-learning screens). None of them could view page pictures, so their layout statements come from the text. Layout in this note was checked by the author's own assistant on contact sheets of sample pages (about 12 pages each) for the 11 PDFs. The five Word files were read as text only, so colour, icons and page layout are unknown for them. The documents are other people's work: nothing is copied here, and no person's name is recorded.

## The good news

Most recurring labels in these documents are already cues: Say, Ask, Slide, Flipchart, Handout, Key point, Trainer notes, Transition, Role play, Game, Case study, Break, Knowledge check. The icon legend page, the lesson time line, the objective per module, materials and preparation as facilitator-only parts, Likert items, action plans and answer keys all have a home. The five older "program" files are nearly a description of the Guide's own model with a different look. The gaps are mostly not in the words a facilitator says but in the **pages around them**: forms, tables, headers, planning kits and reference pages.

## The gaps, most important first

How often is out of the 16 documents; "several" means three to five.

### 1. Forms, worksheets and instruments as designed pages (12 of 16)

- Blank grids and multi-region worksheets for participants, with sized notes areas.
- Tick-box checklists with Who and When columns (planning, equipment, follow-up).
- Rating grids (1 to 5) with an Evidence column; Yes/No checklists; "Met / Did not meet / Comments" observer forms with signature, date and time lines; contracts with signature lines.
- **Self-scored instruments:** answer, add the columns, fill in a bar graph or scoring grid, read the interpretation (the team-model guide, a conflict survey, the assessment matrix in the long guide).
- Handouts are numbered by unit (Handout 5.4) or by letter (Form B), cited from the outline ("Forms used" column) and from the script ("turn to page 31"), printed in landscape or portrait, and often **tied to a print specification** (copies per person or per team, paper size, editable or not, download link).
- Source credit and permission lines on handouts; reading handouts several pages long.
- Template pages with a fixed shape: a two-by-two "windowpane", a force-field page (driving against restraining), a note-taking grid, an action table (who, what, when, evidence), a scavenger-hunt table, a commitment card printed twice to a page for cutting apart.
- A **designed evaluation form** (header fields, agree-disagree statements, quality and knowledge scales, comment lines) and a training-records sign-off sheet, which the Guide's Likert items and Evaluation cue cover as questions but not as a page.

The Guide's workbook has writing lines and facilitator-only text left out. It has no tables with filled-in structure, no checkboxes, no scoring, no numbering scheme, no page references.

### 2. Time is shown six different ways, and not as minutes alone

Ranges per segment and module ("15 to 20 minutes", module totals); a clock start plus a duration, with set-up before the first segment and clean-up after the last; durations in a parenthesis after a clock time; hours and days per module in a five-day programme; notional hours and credits for a qualification; outline minutes with no stated total (five programmes add up to 5 to 8 hours and never say so); sub-timings inside one step (several). Variants (two-day, one-day, half-day) appear as separate agenda tables with different ranges and different modules; one guide sets a 7-hour and a 2-hour agenda side by side with break and lunch rows and tags content as introductory or intermediate. Some timing depends on **group size** (one guide repeats a rule of thumb: ten minutes for a group of four to six, two more for each extra person). A multi-day agenda needs a start time for each day. Agenda lines can also carry an **interaction mode** (whole group, small group, solo, break) with a colour key.

### 3. A header for each segment and each module

- A TIME / PURPOSE / MATERIALS strip at the top of each segment (the design-workshop guide, 25 times).
- A module fact page: objectives, key message, methodology, a topics-and-durations table with a total, teaching aids (the five-day guide, 5 times).
- A method tag per activity (a dozen kinds in one program) feeding an outline table with Activity, Time, Method and Forms Used columns (all five program files).
- An **optional** flag on a segment or activity.

The Guide has an objective and a time line. Purpose, materials, method and optional have no field.

One guide opens each of its nine units with a box listing the introduction, the preparation and materials, the time, **the unit's participant handouts and its slides** (22 handouts and 47 slides in all, numbered unit.number and cross-referenced from the steps). Those lists, and the references to them, should be built from the guide, not typed. Another asks the facilitator to **choose n of m** blocks (two of five case studies) and says how to choose.

### 4. Slide-linked scripts need a choice of layout

Four different arrangements appear: the slide picture above the notes with Key message, Trainer notes and Transition labels (the long guide, about 230 times); the slide thumbnail **beside** the script, under a segment heading (the design-workshop guide); full-page slides printed in landscape inside a portrait guide (the standards guide); and a slide number in the margin beside each paragraph (the team-model guide). The Guide imports slides as blocks with title, notes and picture. It does not say where the picture goes, and it has no module tab, no restarting slide numbers and no full-page slide print.

### 5. Script mechanics

- **Expected answers:** an Answer or "listen for" line after an Ask, and a Debrief label (the long guide about 18 times; the team-model guide 13 times; the hypothetical-case answer lists in three programs).
- **Option:** substitute or extra material.
- **Spoken lines and stage directions in one paragraph** (quotation marks plus bracketed directions), which the Say/Ask model splits.
- Colour carrying meaning (blue for what to say, red for what to do) in the micro-training guide.
- "Add your own example here" slots, which the Review might read as unfinished.
- A tip box with several options.
- A convention for reading a speaker's notes: plain text is spoken, bold is copied from the slide, italic is a direction.
- "Show slide 3, or a pre-prepared flip chart": a slide with a flip-chart alternative as one instruction.
- A library of named techniques with numbered steps (Think-Pair-Share, Gallery Walk, Parking Lot, Exit Ticket, 3-2-1). The Guide has an activity library; the starter set could include these classics.

### 6. Reference pages

Glossary or definitions list; references and bibliography; inline citations; footnotes and endnotes; numbered figure captions with a source line; pull quotes with attribution; a licence, copyright or permission page; back cover; "designed by" credit lines; a review date and jurisdiction for legal or health content that dates. Several of the longer documents, and a few of the shorter, need most of this.

### 7. The planning kit

Planning timeline in stages (four to eight weeks out through after the event) with tick-box tasks and a lead time, an owner and a notes column; participant **pre-work** and **follow-up** as items of their own; technology and materials tables with a responsibility column (presenter or organiser); materials lists grouped for facilitators, each person and each team, with quantities; room set-up and seating diagrams, with do and don't lists; a grid of facilitation roles (lead, table lead, timekeeper); a follow-up roadmap running to week 26; pre-work; invitation and notification templates; a frequently-asked-objections section; attendance register, code of conduct and service agreement.

### 8. Page and section layout

Numbered full-page section dividers and unit title pages; running headers naming the section; a module tab; mirrored footers; a margin column of icons with labels and a vertical page tab; landscape pages inside a portrait document; Roman numbers for front matter and Arabic for the body; decimal heading numbers with an automatic contents; a one-page learner summary printed rotated (front page and back page).

### 9. Cards and group mechanics

Role cards handed out secretly; match-up cards (question and answer on separate cards); prepared question cards; a jigsaw handout split across groups; rotation rules (count off, triads, rounds); fishbowl and debate with side swaps; observers with plus and minus columns. Today these are prose steps. A structured group field (size, how formed, rounds, roles) plus a cards element would hold them.

### 10. Other outputs

A self-study deck (landscape, one idea per slide, a click-to-reveal knowledge check) is the deliverable in one example. A participants' guide that is a copy of every slide with room for notes is mentioned in another. Both are outputs a program of its own could make; neither is possible from a Word-only output.

## Problems in the examples that a Review could catch

- A contents page with several entries showing a "bookmark not found" message, and entries for sections that are not there.
- Agenda names that differ from the activity names, and typed clock times that do not add up to the stated day (a "9 to 3" workshop whose listed days run four and six and a half hours); a guide that says "one hour" in one place and "two hours" in another.
- A reference to a worksheet that is not in the document.
- A stale document title in the properties ("Aids"); a wrong language flag (Japanese on an English guide); only 5 of the 11 PDFs are tagged for accessibility, and none of the older Word files can be checked from text.
- Placeholder dashes and text left over from another course.
- A total programme length that is never stated, and no breaks scheduled in a six-hour day.
- Content that dates (health numbers, laws and court cases) with no review date.

## What this changes in the plan (`STANDALONE-APP-PLAN-2026-11-13.md`)

- **Section 6, the editing surface:** the author's open question was whether tables, pictures and comments are day-one. The evidence says **tables and fill-in elements are day one** (checkboxes, blank lines and grids, signature and date lines), **pictures and captions** too, and **footnotes** soon. Comments and tracked changes do not appear anywhere.
- **Outputs:** the layouts above (thumbnail beside the script, notes page, dividers, running headers, margin icons, landscape sections) are properties of an output "look", not of the content. That is a reason to make the guide's own layout engine (web page and PDF) the main output and Word one more export.
- **The model:** lesson and segment headers (purpose, materials, method, range, optional), a Forms element with numbering and print specification, expected-answer and Option cues, and a reference set (glossary, references, figures).

## Proposed order, cheapest and most valuable first

1. **Forms as a thing:** numbered handouts with blank lines, tables, checkboxes and signature or date lines; a "Forms used" column on the outline; page and number cross-references; a print specification.
2. **Segment header fields:** purpose, materials, method, minute ranges, optional; the module fact page.
3. **Script:** an expected-answer line after Ask, Debrief, Option, and the optional flag.
4. **Time:** ranges, set-up and clean-up segments, a stated programme total, breaks.
5. **Reference pages:** glossary, references, figure captions and source credits, pull quote, footnotes.
6. **Planning kit:** checklist and timeline templates, a grouped materials list with quantities, a room set-up picture and list, a roles grid.
7. **Layouts** in the program's own outputs.
8. **Self-scored instruments and cards.**
